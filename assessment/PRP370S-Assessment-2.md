## 1. Agile Team Charter

**What it is:** A living agreement that sets out the team's purpose, shared values, roles, working agreements and boundaries.

**Evidence to attach:** The actual team charter you participated in creating. The existing [portfolio README](../README.md#agile-team-charter) already contains a general explanation; attach the real charter as evidence if available.

**My reflection (complete in your own words):**
- **Situation:** What project and team were involved?
- **Task:** What were you personally responsible for?
- **Action:** How did you help establish or follow team agreements?
- **Result:** What did you learn, and how did the charter influence the work?

## 2. User Stories and Tasks

**What it is:** User stories describe software features from an end user's perspective. Tasks break each story into actionable work.

**Evidence to attach:** Screenshots, a document, or a Trello link showing the real user stories and their tasks.

**Suggested evidence format (fill in with real stories):**

| Story ID | As a… | I want… | So that… | Tasks | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [ID] | [user role] | [goal] | [benefit] | [implementation and testing tasks] | [status] |

**My reflection (complete in your own words):** How did you identify user needs, divide stories into tasks, and handle changes or difficulties? What did you learn?

## 3. Product Backlog

**What it is:** A prioritised list of product features, improvements, fixes and other required work.

**Evidence to attach:** Your actual product backlog, preferably showing priorities and current statuses. A Trello screenshot or board link is suitable if it shows your work.

## 5. Agile Logbook

**What it is:** A dated record of planning, stand-ups, blockers, progress and retrospective notes.

**Evidence to attach:** Your real dated logbook entries or screenshots. Do not invent meetings, dates or contributions.

| Date | Activity | Progress made | Blockers | Next action |

**My reflection (complete in your own words):** How did keeping the logbook help you track work and communicate? What patterns or improvements did you notice?

## 6. Collect, Select, Reflect, Connect

The assessment brief identifies four steps for an e-portfolio:

1. **Collect:** Gather your actual project artefacts.
2. **Select:** Choose the artefacts that best demonstrate your learning.
3. **Reflect:** Explain what you did, what you learned and what changed.
4. **Connect:** Link your evidence and reflections into a coherent portfolio.

Useful evidence may include presentation videos, audio, assignments, group-project artefacts, slides, photographs, websites, timelines and diagrams. Only include items you can explain and substantiate.
